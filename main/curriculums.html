In 2021, the world's oceans were hotter than ever recorded, even though surface air temperatures ranked only fifth or sixth warmest. In an interview, climate scientist Kevin Trenberth, a coauthor of a 2022 study by 23 researchers at 14 institutes, explained why ocean heat is a better measure of global warming and how it supercharged storms, floods, heat waves, and wildfires.

Unpacking the Climate Hiatus: Where Did the Global Warming Heat Go?

Between 2009 and 2014, several harsh winters in Europe and the eastern United States led people to ask, "Where is global warming?" Climate scientist Kevin Trenberth explained that these cold spots covered only about 2% of the globe, while the Arctic was unusually warm. His research traced the pattern to the tropical Pacific, where changes in the ocean and atmosphere sent waves through the jet stream and carried heat deep into the ocean.

Exploring Climate Vulnerability in Coulibistrie, Dominica

Coulibistrie is a village of about 423 people on the west coast of Dominica, tucked into a river valley between steep mountains and the Caribbean Sea. After Tropical Storm Erika in 2015 and Hurricane Maria in 2017, residents are still rebuilding. Researchers used community sessions and geospatial mapping to understand why some places struggle to recover and how neighbors, local businesses, and outside partners can build resilience together.

Building Community-Driven Climate Resilience in West Bengal

In 2020, a cyclone struck West Bengal, India, causing $13 billion in damage. For coastal villages, storms like this close schools, spread disease, ruin farmland, and harm mental health. Harvard Chan C-CHANGE and local partners are developing a community-driven approach to climate resilience that starts by listening to residents, especially young people, and then designs and tests solutions with them.
